# Product lens
Score: 5 | Vote: FUND IF
## What is strong
The pain is concrete and product-shaped: people already assemble lists from Instagram and scattered sources, and Wibe offers ready-made, kept-up-to-date collections they can use immediately. Free for end users lowers the barrier to the first session. Editorial curation plus suggest-to-add is a coherent content model — the product owns taste instead of dumping an empty UGC box on the user. Shipping a built Persian RTL web app means the board is judging a real experience surface, not a slide. The one-sentence pitch is almost there: tidy curated lists so Iranians stop rebuilding the same collections themselves.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No single “one thing.”** Film, series, books, podcasts, travel, restaurants, and cafés is seven products wearing one logo. The Product lens kills ideas that refuse to say no. Taste and craft cannot be brilliant across cinema *and* cafés at once; the home screen will feel like a directory, and the first five minutes will not answer one sharp job (“what do I watch tonight?” or “where do I eat this weekend?”). Sprawl dilutes the experience until nothing feels finished.
2. **Monetization seams that break trust.** Ads, sponsorships, and outbound links to shops sit inside the same lists that promise clean, up-to-date curation. If a sponsored or affiliate item reads louder than editorial judgment — or if links feel like the real product and the list is packaging — the experience hands off from “trusted collection” to “ad inventory.” Seams are where products die; this one is baked into the revenue path.
3. **Stale or thin lists kill the promise in session one.** The brief stakes the product on “kept-up-to-date.” A ready app with thin, outdated, or Instagram-scraped-looking lists fails the first five minutes: the user opens a list, smells unfinished craft, and never returns. Without a visible freshness and quality bar, “curated” is marketing copy, not product.

## The questions I would ask the founder
1. In one sentence, what is the *one* thing Wibe must do brilliantly — and which categories would you cut for the next twelve months to protect that?
2. Walk the first five minutes cold: a new user lands with no account. What exact job do they finish before they leave, and what has to be true in the content and UI for that moment to feel inevitable?
3. Where does the experience hand off — editorial ingest → published list → sponsor/outbound link → user suggestion review — and what goes wrong at each seam when money or volume pressure rises?
4. What is the craft standard for a list that ships (item count, recency, editorial voice) versus a list that stays draft? Who says no?
5. How often must a “live” list be touched to still count as up-to-date, and what does the user see when it is not? (Open: retention and return behavior — `/founder-consumer`.)
6. When an outbound or sponsored item appears, how does the product still feel like one owned experience rather than a handoff to someone else’s checkout? (Open: willingness to tolerate ads vs. abandon — `/founder-consumer`; unit economics of ads/sponsors/affiliates — `/founder-cfo`.)
7. Is discovery (content, social, search) part of the product surface — shareable list pages, deep links, category entry — or an afterthought bolted on after launch? (Open: who else owns this habit in-reach — `/founder-competitors`.)

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Cut until one job is unmistakable.** Ship (or publicly lead with) one primary vertical — or at most a tight pair that share the same use moment — until that experience is undeniable; park the rest behind a clear “later” rather than equal-weight nav.
2. **Name the first-five-minutes outcome in one sentence** and design the home path only for that outcome (not a category mall). Prove it with real users before expanding scope (`/founder-consumer`).
3. **Write non-negotiable editorial rules that monetization cannot override** — how sponsors and outbound links appear, when they are refused, and how the list still feels curated after the click path.
4. **Define a freshness and craft bar** (what “up-to-date” means per list type, and what users see when a list slips) so the ready product cannot ship hollow collections.
5. **Map every experience seam** (ingest, publish, suggest review, ad/sponsor slot, outbound) and own the ones the user sees; no silent quality drop where “nobody looks.”
