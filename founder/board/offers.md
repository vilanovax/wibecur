# Offers lens
Score: 5 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong

The user-facing value equation is unusually clean for a free product. Dream outcome is concrete: leave Instagram scavenger hunts and own sorting behind; open ready-made, tidy, kept-up-to-date lists across film, series, books, podcasts, travel, restaurants and cafés. Time-to-result can be near-zero (browse and use). Effort and sacrifice are low (optional suggestions only; no paywall). Perceived likelihood gets a real boost from editorial/ingest curation plus a suggestion funnel — that is more believable than “another empty UGC list app.”

Pain selection is market-shaped, not feature-shaped: people who already try to collect from Instagram and scattered sources, and who hate maintaining those piles. That is a recurring frustration, not a one-time curiosity.

Product is already built. From the Offers lens, that means the offer can be pressure-tested on live usage and proof instead of deck fiction — scarcity of capital to “build” is not the bottleneck; clarity of who pays and why is.

Free-to-user + ads/sponsors/outbound links is a coherent *structure* if (and only if) the free side creates dense, habitual attention worth buying. The stack on the free side can deepen without charging users: category depth, freshness signals, “last updated,” outbound usefulness to shops/pages, suggestion → inclusion loop. Those are cheap-to-valuable levers if ops hold.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)

1. **No solvent paying market for the free audience.** End users pay zero. The Offers lens scores markets on pain *and* money *and* reachability. Here the money sits with advertisers, sponsors, and outbound/affiliate partners — and that paid offer is still “open / not fully decided.” If sponsors will not buy this inventory (wrong audience density, wrong categories for Iran ad budgets, weak outbound conversion, or unproven engagement), the user love does not become a business. Market failure on the *payer* side kills free products faster than a weak landing page.

2. **Likelihood collapses on “curated and kept up to date.”** That phrase is the whole grand-slam claim. If lists feel stale, thin, or like a dump of Instagram screenshots, perceived likelihood of the dream outcome tanks; effort stays low but users stop returning. No return visits → no ad/sponsor inventory → monetization dies even if acquisition worked once.

3. **Commodity “free lists” with nothing that makes saying no feel stupid.** Instagram saves, Telegram channels, friends’ shares, and search already approximate “lists” for free. If Wibe’s only edge is “also free, also lists,” it competes as a commodity on the wrong axis. Without a stacked outcome (faster than DIY, more trustworthy than random channels, more complete than personal saves — with proof), distribution spend (even organic content effort) buys churn, not a durable offer.

## The questions I would ask the founder

1. Who exactly is the *paying* buyer in the first 12 months — local brands, category sponsors (e.g. cafés/travel), affiliate merchants, display networks — and what do they buy (placement, list sponsorship, outbound click, package)? Open: needs `/founder-pricing` and `/founder-cfo`.
2. What proof will a first-time user see in five minutes that these lists are fresher and better than their Instagram saves? (Updated-at, editorial standard, coverage depth — not slogans.)
3. How fast does a cold visitor get a usable result in one category they care about, and what is the return habit you are selling them (weekly reopen? trip planning? “what should I watch tonight”)? Open: `/founder-consumer`.
4. For the free side: what would make someone feel stupid staying on DIY Instagram lists — more outcome, less effort, speed, or a guarantee of freshness? Name one.
5. For the paid side: could you raise what a sponsor pays and still be the obvious buy vs. Instagram ads or Telegram channel sponsorships — or are you only cheaper inventory? Open: `/founder-competitors` and `/founder-pricing`.
6. Is the Iran attention market for these categories growing in a way that favors a dedicated list destination, or are you fishing in a flat/fragmented pool? Do not invent size — answer with `/founder-competitors` + `/founder-consumer`.
7. What is the minimum engagement / outbound metric at which ads or sponsor packages clear a floor that keeps ops (curation labor) funded? Open: `/founder-cfo`.
8. Which problems in the journey (find list → trust it → act → return → maybe click out) are already solved vs. still missing from the stack — and which of those are cheap for you and valuable for the user or sponsor?

## If FUND IF: the conditions

1. **Write the paid offer before scaling the free one.** Name the first paying customer type, the unit they buy, a target price band, and why that buyer feels stupid saying no vs. existing Iran channels. No invented CPMs — validate with `/founder-pricing` / `/founder-cfo`; map alternatives with `/founder-competitors`.
2. **Make “kept up to date” operational and visible.** Define a freshness SLA per category, show it in product, and prove retention/return behavior with real users (`/founder-consumer`) so the free value equation’s likelihood term is not a hope.
3. **Pick a grand-slam wedge, not seven equal categories.** Dominate one high-intent, sponsor-friendly slice first (where outbound or sponsorship naturally fits), then expand — so the stack is deep where money and pain meet, not thin everywhere.
4. **Confirm the free audience is findable at sustainable effort.** Content/social/search is stated, not measured. Until CAC/effort-to-habit is known (`/founder-consumer`, unit economics via `/founder-cfo`), treat growth as a condition, not a given.
