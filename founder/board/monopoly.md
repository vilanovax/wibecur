# Monopoly lens
Score: 4 | Vote: FUND IF
## What is strong
- The product is already built, so the bet is on market structure and distribution, not on whether software can exist.
- There is a plausible *small* beachhead: Persian speakers in Iran who want ready, kept-up-to-date lists instead of assembling them from Instagram and scattered sources — a narrower slice than “everyone who discovers culture online.”
- Editorial curation plus user suggestions is a concrete operating model, not a pure UGC free-for-all; if the lists are meaningfully better than the next option on something users feel (freshness, taste, completeness, trust), that is the only path toward proprietary know-how that matters.
- A free end-user product with ads, sponsors, and outbound links fits a media/monopoly-of-attention logic: if one destination becomes the default place for a category in this language and geography, monetization can concentrate; without that share, the model is weak by design.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No durable advantage — competition and substitutes eat the margin.** Curated lists are easy to copy; Instagram, Telegram channels, blogs, and other list surfaces already sit in the same habit loop. Without roughly 10× better on something that matters, network effects, scale advantages, or a brand that actually owns a category, this becomes a crowded free-content fight where ads and sponsors pay thin rates. Competitor set, substitutes, and switching costs are not in the brief — open question for `/founder-competitors`.
2. **Starting too broad to own a small market.** Seven categories (film, series, books, podcasts, travel, restaurants, cafés) from day one looks like a horizontal portal, not a monopoly wedge. The Monopoly lens wants a big share of a *small* market first, then expansion. Spreading editorial effort across everything risks being second-best everywhere and first nowhere.
3. **Distribution and monetization without ownership of attention.** Revenue depends on ads, sponsorships, and links — all of which require concentrated traffic and trust. A good product with no repeatable way to reach the first cohorts is not a business. CAC, first-100 acquisition path, and whether advertisers will pay for this audience are missing — open questions for `/founder-consumer` (reach and habit) and `/founder-cfo` (unit economics of ads/sponsors/links).

## The questions I would ask the founder
1. **Monopoly / wedge:** What is the *smallest* market you can credibly dominate in 12–24 months (e.g. one category + one use case), and what share would count as “owned”?
2. **Engineering:** On what dimension are the lists ~10× better than Instagram/Telegram roundups for the same buyer — freshness, selection, structure, trust, speed — and how would a stranger verify that in five minutes?
3. **Secret:** What do you know about this audience or this content pipeline that others building the same app would not?
4. **Timing:** Why is now the moment this becomes the default, rather than another list site people try once?
5. **Durability:** In ten years, what still stops a well-funded copycat or a platform feature from making Wibe optional?
6. **Distribution:** How do the first 100, then 10٬000, weekly users arrive, and what does each cohort cost? (Numbers needed from `/founder-consumer` / `/founder-cfo`.)
7. **Competition:** Who else sells or gives away this within reach of the same Iranian buyer today, and how do they monetize? (Map via `/founder-competitors`.)
8. **Brand:** If the logo were removed, would regulars still know they were on “the” place for that category — and which category is that?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Pick one beachhead category (or one tightly coupled pair) and prove category ownership** before treating all seven as equal. Expansion only after a defensible share of that small market — not after “we also have restaurants.”
2. **Name the secret and the 10× claim in writing**, with a falsifiable test against the status quo (Instagram/Telegram/scattered sources). If neither exists, do not scale spend.
3. **Show a distribution path that is not hope:** a repeatable channel to the beachhead audience, plus early evidence that sponsors/ads/links can attach to that attention. Bring rough CAC and willingness-to-pay-from-advertisers via `/founder-consumer` and `/founder-cfo`; map substitutes via `/founder-competitors`.
4. **Design for a moat, not just a catalog:** deepen proprietary editorial process, data, or habits (e.g. suggestion → curation loop that compounds quality) so month-six copycats face something harder than cloning pages.
